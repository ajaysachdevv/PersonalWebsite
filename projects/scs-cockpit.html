Note 1: The problemThree ways to fail

The Strategic Case Study is the last CIMA exam: a fictional company you've studied for weeks, a clock, and a run of emails asking what you'd actually do. Knowing the theory isn't the hard part. The guidance names three ways people fail: generic theory that could apply to any company, no real recommendation, and missing the facts the scenario hands you.

Most study material tests what you know. I wanted something that tested how I answered, under time, against those three.

Failure modeWhat the tool does about it
Generic theoryEvery drill is anchored to the case company, and Application is marked on whether the answer used its numbers
No recommendationJudgement is marked on a clear call, not a balanced menu of options
Missing scenario factsThe command verb is flagged before you write, with the shape of answer it needs
Note 2: How it worksSeeds, verbs, a clock

A drill is composed, not pulled from a bank: a case-specific scenario seed, a competency, a template and an anchor from the study material. It arrives as an email with tasks attached, and a timer changes colour as time runs down. Afterwards the answer is scored on the five dimensions the exam marks: role fit, application, judgement, structure and ethics.

Behind it, 54 source PDFs were normalised into 166 study items and 23 frameworks, loaded from three packs and de-duplicated at startup. Spaced repetition decides what comes back tomorrow. One button turns any drill into an examiner prompt I could hand to an outside AI for a second opinion.

Note 4: What went wrongThe mock I never built

The exam is three hours under one clock, and the feature that would have rehearsed exactly that never got built. Mock exam mode, three linked sections, a three-hour clock and a structured debrief, went to the top of the priority list in April. It was still at the top after the exam. I built short timed drills instead. They worked, but they weren't the whole exam.

The highest-value feature on the list stayed on the list.
